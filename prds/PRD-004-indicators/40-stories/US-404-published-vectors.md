As the owner, I want RSI and ATR checked against numbers published by someone else, before we
existed, so that "correct" means agreeing with the outside world rather than with ourselves.

**This is the PRD's adversarial case.** US-006 passed 6/6 while storing a Hong Kong day close; a
golden generated by our own code would have made it permanent. StockCharts publish downloadable
worked spreadsheets for exactly this purpose, and they are variant-specific enough that a
Wilder-vs-EMA mistake cannot slip through.

Wilder's recursion, verbatim from the source:
`Average Gain = [(previous Average Gain) x 13 + current Gain] / 14`
`Current ATR = [(Prior ATR x 13) + Current TR] / 14`

## Acceptance criteria

- [test: RSI(14) reproduces the published worked-example output on its published input series] Committed fixture, exact input, expected value cited to its source
- [test: ATR(14) reproduces the published worked-example output] Same discipline
- [test: each golden names its external source and is marked external] A golden without provenance fails, as PRD-002 established
- [test: an EMA-smoothed RSI variant fails the published vector] Proves the test discriminates between conventions rather than merely passing
- [test: the same tail computed at N=250 and N=500 agrees within epsilon] Convergence, separate from formula correctness — the two are different claims
- [cmd: uv run pytest tests/test_published_vectors.py -q --no-header -o addopts= --tb=short] The vector suite runs on its own

## Notes for the implementer

- The publisher warns their own spreadsheet "may not match exactly with what is seen on the
  price chart" for a small subset of data — so pin the **spreadsheet** values at the
  spreadsheet's length, and test convergence separately at 250. Conflating the two produces a
  test that fails for the right reason and the wrong one indistinguishably.
- Keep the vectors short enough that a human can check the arithmetic by hand.
